Photosynthesis is a biological process used by plants, algae, and certain bacteria to convert light energy into chemical energy.
The process occurs in two main stages:
1. Light-Dependent Reactions: Take place in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs solar energy and splits water molecules into oxygen, protons, and electrons. Oxygen is released as a byproduct, and ATP and NADPH are synthesized.
2. Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle): Take place in the stroma of chloroplasts. ATP and NADPH produced in the light stage are used to fix carbon dioxide into glucose (C6H12O6).

Key Equation:
6 CO2 + 6 H2O + Light Energy -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2

Importance:
Photosynthesis provides the primary energy source for nearly all ecosystems on Earth and generates the oxygen required for aerobic cellular respiration.
